# Executive Summary

## OPT-In Family Services Data Quality and Outcomes Dashboard

This work sample uses fully synthetic data to model a multi-state family support program serving families screened out from a child protective services hotline and referred to community-based prevention services. The project demonstrates how referral, outreach, engagement, flexible fund, service, and outcome data can be cleaned and translated into practical insights for program staff and leadership.

## Key Findings

- The cleaned analytic file includes 739 validated family referral records after removing invalid dates and duplicate family IDs from 774 raw synthetic rows.
- The overall engagement rate is 60.9%, with an outreach completion rate of 66.3% and an average of 2.3 outreach attempts per referral.
- Follow-up completion is 64.4%, making it a useful operational signal for whether staff can confirm service connection, family decline, or unresolved need.
- The project identifies $111,931.60 in synthetic flexible funds distributed across sites, with Illinois and Texas accounting for the largest totals.
- 106 records are flagged for case review, helping program teams prioritize families with complex outreach, unresolved follow-up, or active service status.

## Program Interpretation

The dashboard is designed to help program teams answer four practical questions:

1. Are referred families being reached?
2. Are families engaging with community-based services?
3. Which services and supports are most frequently provided?
4. Where do data quality issues limit confidence in the findings?

These questions are intentionally operational. They support monthly learning conversations with site teams, rather than one-time compliance reporting.

## Recommended Actions

- Review outreach completion and engagement rates by site during monthly implementation meetings.
- Use case review flags to prioritize families with multiple outreach attempts or unresolved follow-up.
- Monitor flexible fund distributions for equity, consistency, and alignment with family needs.
- Standardize required referral fields across sites, especially site, referral date, follow-up status, service type, and outcome status.
- Use the data quality report as a feedback loop for local staff training and data entry improvements.
