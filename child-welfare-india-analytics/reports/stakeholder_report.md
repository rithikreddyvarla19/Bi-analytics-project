# Stakeholder Report

## Purpose

This report summarizes insights from a synthetic child welfare support services dataset covering ten Indian states. It is intended for program managers, operations leads, monitoring and evaluation teams, and analytics stakeholders who need practical signals for service planning.

## Executive Takeaway

The synthetic program dataset shows a substantial service footprint, with **9,771 clean cases**, a **28.47% high/critical risk share**, and a **58.44% follow-up completion rate**. The strongest opportunity for program improvement is not only case intake visibility, but also continuity after initial contact.

## Demand and Referral Patterns

Case volume is concentrated in Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka, and West Bengal. Childline/Helpline is the largest referral source, followed by schools and community workers. This pattern suggests that centralized intake and community-based identification channels are both important parts of the support network.

Recommended action:

- Maintain referral source reporting as a standing dashboard view.
- Use referral source trends to guide outreach partnerships with schools, helplines, health facilities, and NGOs.
- Monitor whether high-risk referrals are concentrated in specific sources or geographies.

## Risk and Response Time

High and critical cases make up **28.47%** of records. Average first-contact time across all clean cases is **4.56 days**. A single average, however, can hide whether urgent cases are being contacted quickly enough.

Recommended action:

- Set separate first-contact targets by risk level.
- Review high-risk and critical cases daily or weekly, depending on program capacity.
- Add exception reporting for cases that exceed the target contact window.

## Service Utilization

Counseling is the most frequently recorded service, followed by nutrition support, education kits, medical referrals, and family counseling. This suggests a mix of psychosocial, material, health, and family-based support needs.

Recommended action:

- Review service utilization by state to understand resource demand.
- Compare service mix against outcomes to identify which service pathways may need deeper evaluation.
- Track repeat outreach attempts to understand where engagement barriers are emerging.

## Follow-up and Closure

The follow-up completion rate is **58.44%**, while the overall closure rate is **48.85%**. In a real program setting, this gap would warrant review because cases may receive an initial service but still require additional monitoring, documentation, or referral closure.

Recommended action:

- Build a follow-up aging report by state, district, and risk level.
- Assign ownership for pending and in-progress follow-ups.
- Use closure rate by risk level as a program management indicator, not as a standalone measure of success.

## Outcome Monitoring

The most common outcome category is `Stabilized`, followed by `Improved Access to Services` and `Ongoing Support Needed`. Outcomes should be interpreted carefully because they depend on follow-up quality and verification practices.

Recommended action:

- Standardize outcome definitions.
- Require outcome updates for closed cases.
- Track `Unable to Verify Outcome` as a data and program quality indicator.

## Dashboard Recommendations

The dashboard should support three stakeholder needs:

- **Leadership view:** total cases, high-risk percentage, follow-up completion, closure rate, and monthly trend.
- **Operations view:** state, district, referral source, risk level, first-contact time, and follow-up aging.
- **Program evaluation view:** service utilization, outcome category, support amount distribution, and closure by risk level.

## Limitations

This analysis is based on synthetic data created for portfolio demonstration. It should not be used to assess actual child welfare conditions, public service performance, NGO operations, or government programs in India.
