# Executive Summary

## Child Welfare Support Services Analytics - India

This analysis reviews a fully synthetic dataset of child welfare support service referrals across ten Indian states. The project was designed to demonstrate how program data can be cleaned, validated, summarized, and translated into decision-ready reporting for child welfare operations.

**Data notice:** The dataset is entirely synthetic and does not contain any real child, family, NGO, or government records.

## Key Findings

The cleaned dataset includes **9,771 analysis-ready case records** after removing duplicate case IDs and invalid referral dates. Case demand is highest in Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka, and West Bengal, reflecting the synthetic distribution used to model larger program catchments.

High or critical risk cases represent **28.47%** of the clean dataset. This indicates that nearly three in ten referrals require closer prioritization, faster outreach, or escalation pathways. The average time to first contact is **4.56 days**, which is acceptable for lower-risk referrals but may require tighter monitoring for high-risk and critical cases.

Follow-up completion is **58.44%**, suggesting an opportunity to improve case continuity after initial service delivery. The overall closure rate is **48.85%**, with closure rates broadly similar across risk levels in the synthetic data. Counseling, nutrition support, education kits, and medical referrals are the most frequently used services.

## Program Interpretation

The synthetic reporting pattern suggests three operational priorities:

1. Improve follow-up completion through clearer ownership, contact protocols, and escalation rules.
2. Monitor high-risk and critical cases separately from general case volume.
3. Use state-level response time and service utilization dashboards to identify where additional field capacity or partner coordination may be needed.

## Recommended Next Steps

- Create a weekly operational review dashboard for case intake, response time, risk level, and follow-up completion.
- Add service-level targets for first contact, especially for high-risk and critical referrals.
- Track unresolved cases by state, district, referral source, and risk level.
- Use outcome distribution as a program evaluation signal, while documenting limitations in outcome verification.
- Maintain a recurring data quality report to monitor duplicate IDs, missing geography, invalid dates, and inconsistent status labels.
